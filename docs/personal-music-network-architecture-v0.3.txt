PERSONAL MUSIC NETWORK (PMN)
Complete Architecture Concept
Version: 0.3
Language: English

======================================================================
1. OVERVIEW
======================================================================

Personal Music Network (PMN) is a user-centered music computing
architecture.

It is not merely a music player, a synchronization service, a streaming
client, a recommendation engine, or a multi-device audio system. It is a
logical music environment in which music can be acquired, understood,
organized, planned, transformed, rendered, distributed, synchronized,
computed, and experienced across one or many devices.

The central idea is:

    Music should be modeled first.
    Distribution comes second.

A PMN must therefore remain complete and coherent when only one physical
device exists, while also scaling naturally to multiple devices without
changing the logical meaning of the system.

The architecture separates musical intent from physical execution.

The execution chain is:

    1. Music Planning
       Determines how music should happen.

    2. Capability Requirement Derivation
       Determines what abstract capabilities are required to realize the
       musical plan.

    3. Capability Planning
       Matches those requirements against available capability instances and
       constructs a placement graph.

    4. Network Scheduling
       Maps the placement graph onto real nodes, links, artifacts, clocks,
       and resource states.

In compact form:

    User Intent
        |
        v
    Music Planner
        |
        v
    Logical Musical Timeline
        |
        v
    Capability Requirements
        |
        v
    Capability Planner
        |
        v
    Placement Graph
        |
        v
    Network Scheduler
        |
        v
    Distributed Execution Graph
        |
        v
    Capability Instances on Physical Nodes
        |
        v
    Listening Experience
        |
        +---------------------> Feedback / Learning

The key design principles are:

    The logical music system is independent of the number and type of devices.

    Personal sovereignty is a first-class architectural boundary.

A single-node PMN is still a PMN.
A multi-node PMN is the same logical PMN whose required capabilities happen
to be placed across multiple physical nodes.

Nodes are not the primary scheduling abstraction.
Capabilities are.

A PMN is also not a globally readable user profile.

Public music knowledge may be shared and federated.
Personal behavior, preferences, memory, private resources, credentials, and
representations derived from them remain inside the owner's PMN trust
boundary by default.

Cross-PMN interaction must therefore be explicit, scoped, and minimal.


======================================================================
2. FIRST PRINCIPLE: DECOMPOSE "MUSIC"
======================================================================

The word "music" in Personal Music Network must not be reduced to
"track files".

The complete music lifecycle is:

    Music Source
        ->
    Acquisition
        ->
    Identification / Understanding
        ->
    Music Knowledge
        ->
    Selection
        ->
    Sequencing
        ->
    Transition Planning
        ->
    Timeline Compilation
        ->
    Audio Rendering
        ->
    Output
        ->
    Listening Feedback
        ->
    Personal Learning

This lifecycle separates several fundamentally different questions:

    Where does the music come from?
    What is this music?
    What should be played?
    In what order should it be played?
    How should neighboring musical regions connect?
    How should the final continuous timeline be rendered?
    Where should computation and playback execute?
    How does the user's behavior change future planning?

This is the conceptual center of PMN.


======================================================================
3. MUSIC INPUT
======================================================================

3.1 Music Sources

A MusicSource is anything capable of exposing musical material.

Examples include:

    - streaming providers
    - local files
    - cloud drives
    - NAS storage
    - peer PMN nodes
    - internet URLs
    - live streams
    - radio
    - physical media
    - microphones
    - user recordings
    - generated audio
    - another federated PMN

A source is not the owner of musical identity.

For example:

    NetEase song ID
    local path
    cloud object ID

are source-specific locators, not canonical music identity.

A source answers:

    What can I expose?
    Can it be accessed now?
    How can it be acquired?

A source does not decide:

    What canonical musical entity is this?
    Should it be played next?
    Where should playback happen?


3.2 Acquisition

Acquisition converts source availability into usable media.

The acquisition layer handles:

    - network requests
    - byte ranges
    - chunk transfer
    - streaming
    - downloading
    - caching
    - peer transfer
    - integrity verification
    - content hashing
    - media probing
    - partial materialization
    - replica discovery
    - expiry information
    - authorization constraints

Streaming, caching, downloading, and replication are therefore treated as
variations of one operation:

    Materialize musical data.

The difference is how much data is materialized, where, and for how long.


======================================================================
4. MUSIC UNDERSTANDING
======================================================================

4.1 Music Perception

After acquisition, PMN should be capable of building a perceptual and
musical representation of the material.

Physical properties may include:

    sample rate
    channel layout
    bit depth
    loudness
    dynamic range
    spectrum

Musical properties may include:

    tempo
    beat
    downbeat
    meter
    key
    chord
    phrase structure
    section boundaries
    timbre
    instrumentation
    vocal presence

High-level semantic properties may include:

    genre
    style
    era
    energy
    valence
    mood
    similarity
    lyrical topic

Identity-related analysis may include:

    song identification
    artist identity
    recording identity
    version identity
    lyric recognition
    language

This layer can use deterministic DSP, symbolic methods, machine learning,
large models, embeddings, or combinations of them.

AI is therefore not an optional chat feature attached to PMN.
It is one possible implementation mechanism of the Music Intelligence
layer.


4.2 Music Knowledge Graph

The Music Knowledge Graph represents what PMN knows about music.

Typical entity types include:

    Work
    Recording
    Version
    Release
    ReleaseTrack
    Artist
    Playlist
    LyricsTrack
    Artwork
    Video
    MediaVariant
    MusicalSegment
    AnalysisArtifact
    UserKnowledge

Relations may include:

    same_as
    version_of
    cover_of
    remix_of
    remaster_of
    live_version_of
    performed_by
    composed_by
    released_as
    belongs_to
    sampled_from
    similar_to
    transition_to
    preferred_after

The graph is a domain model, not a requirement to use a graph database.

SQLite, relational tables, indexes, and materialized views are perfectly
valid storage implementations.


======================================================================
5. MUSICAL SEGMENTS
======================================================================

A Track is too coarse to be the fundamental unit of advanced playback.

PMN introduces MusicalSegment as a first-class concept.

A recording may contain:

    Intro
    Verse
    Chorus
    Bridge
    Breakdown
    Solo
    Outro

Segments may themselves expose:

    beat boundaries
    bar boundaries
    phrase boundaries
    stems
    local tempo
    local key
    local energy
    local musical state

This allows the system to represent:

    "Leave Track A at the end of its second chorus
     and enter Track B at its intro."

instead of only:

    "Play A, then play B."

A MusicalSegment is therefore the practical middle layer between an entire
track and raw audio samples.


======================================================================
6. MUSIC STATE SPACE
======================================================================

PMN models a musical region as a state in a multidimensional music space.

A conceptual MusicState may include:

    tempo
    key
    loudness
    energy
    rhythm density
    timbre
    instrumentation
    vocal presence
    emotional character
    semantic topic
    familiarity
    novelty
    session position

A segment can therefore be mapped to:

    Segment
        ->
    MusicState

Playback becomes the construction of a trajectory through MusicState space.

This is more general than a queue of track IDs.


======================================================================
7. SELECTION
======================================================================

Selection answers:

    What musical material could be played next?

Candidate generation can consider:

    - local library
    - streaming providers
    - peer nodes
    - caches
    - user favorites
    - historical behavior
    - semantic similarity
    - audio similarity
    - current context
    - user intent
    - newly discovered material
    - generated musical material

Selection produces candidates.
It does not yet define a final order.


======================================================================
8. SEQUENCING
======================================================================

A conventional player uses:

    Vec<Track>

as its core playback structure.

PMN instead uses a SessionGraph.

Example:

             C
           /
    A -> B -> D -> F
    |       \
    +-> E    G

Edges are weighted by properties such as:

    transition quality
    user preference
    novelty
    artist repetition
    energy trajectory
    harmonic compatibility
    semantic continuity
    recent-history penalties
    context compatibility

The next musical item is therefore a planning decision, not just an index
increment.


======================================================================
9. SESSION SHAPE
======================================================================

PMN should optimize not only pairwise transitions but the macrostructure of
an entire listening session.

A session may have a shape such as:

    Warmup
        ->
    Build
        ->
    Peak
        ->
    Release
        ->
    Contrast
        ->
    Second Build
        ->
    Ending

A session objective may be expressed as:

    "Gradually become more intense."
    "Wind down over the next hour."
    "Avoid abrupt style changes."
    "Make it feel like a DJ set."
    "Explore more unfamiliar music."
    "Keep the energy stable."

This transforms playback from "choose next track" into long-horizon musical
planning.


======================================================================
10. TRANSITION SPACE
======================================================================

Transitions must not be hard-coded as a small enum such as:

    HardCut
    Crossfade
    BeatMatch

Instead PMN defines a general TransitionSpace(A, B).

A transition solution can include:

    source exit point
    target entry point
    alignment
    duration
    gain curves
    EQ curves
    tempo curves
    pitch curves
    stem curves
    effect curves
    spatial curves
    generated bridge material

A transition is therefore a small music transformation program.

This is one of the most important abstractions in PMN.


======================================================================
11. MUSICAL TIMELINE ALGEBRA
======================================================================

PMN should define a compact set of primitive operations from which complex
playback behavior can be composed.

Candidate primitives include:

    CUT
    CONCAT
    OVERLAP

    SEEK
    TRIM
    LOOP

    GAIN
    PAN
    EQ

    STRETCH
    PITCH

    ALIGN_BEAT
    ALIGN_BAR
    ALIGN_PHRASE

    SELECT_STEM
    MIX

    SPATIALIZE

    GENERATE

Examples:

Crossfade:

    OVERLAP
    + GAIN(A: 1 -> 0)
    + GAIN(B: 0 -> 1)

Beat transition:

    ALIGN_BEAT
    + STRETCH
    + OVERLAP
    + GAIN

Advanced AutoMix:

    ALIGN_PHRASE
    + ALIGN_BEAT
    + STRETCH
    + EQ
    + GAIN

The objective is not to create endless special-case playback features.
The objective is to make those features compositions over one general
timeline algebra.


======================================================================
12. TIMELINE IR
======================================================================

Music Planning should not directly drive the realtime audio callback.

Instead it produces a Music Timeline IR.

Example:

    00:00.000  START A @ 00:14.230
    02:51.200  A gain 1.0 -> 0.0 over 8 bars
    02:51.200  START B @ 00:08.440
    02:51.200  B tempo 126 -> 128
    02:51.200  B gain 0.0 -> 1.0
    03:07.100  STOP A

The pipeline becomes:

    Music Assets
        ->
    Music Planner
        ->
    Timeline IR
        ->
    Timeline Compiler
        ->
    DSP / Mix Graph
        ->
    PCM / Encoded Output

This cleanly separates high-level musical reasoning from realtime audio
execution.


======================================================================
13. PLAYBACK CAPABILITY CHANGES THE MUSIC GRAPH
======================================================================

Playback is not merely a passive backend.

Suppose A and B appear musically incompatible.

If the engine supports:

    tempo morphing
    pitch morphing
    stem isolation
    generated bridge material
    phrase-aware transitions

then an edge that previously did not exist may become feasible.

Therefore:

    Playback Capabilities
        ->
    Transition Feasibility
        ->
    Session Graph Topology

A stronger playback engine increases the number of valid musical paths
available to the planner.


======================================================================
14. GENERATED TRANSITIONS
======================================================================

Generative systems naturally fit into PMN as one more source of timeline
material.

Example:

    Track A
        ->
    Generated 8-bar Bridge
        ->
    Track B

The generated material can satisfy explicit constraints:

    key compatibility
    tempo trajectory
    energy trajectory
    instrumentation compatibility
    duration
    phrase boundaries

Generated content remains a normal MusicAsset with provenance.

PMN should distinguish:

    OriginalArtifact
    DerivedArtifact
    GeneratedArtifact


======================================================================
15. PERSONAL MUSIC MODEL
======================================================================

User modeling should extend beyond "liked tracks".

A complete PersonalMusicModel may include:

    ContentPreference
    SequencePreference
    TransitionPreference
    SessionPreference
    ContextPreference
    DiscoveryPreference

Examples:

    "Likes this artist."
    "Dislikes two songs by the same artist in a row."
    "Prefers hard cuts over long fades."
    "At night, prefers sessions that gradually reduce energy."
    "While running, prefers higher tempo."
    "Accepts approximately 20% unfamiliar music."

This allows PMN to learn how the user prefers music to be organized and
experienced, not only which tracks they like.


======================================================================
16. FEEDBACK LOOP
======================================================================

Playback feeds back into planning.

Events can include:

    started
    paused
    resumed
    seeked
    skipped
    replayed
    completed
    manually_selected
    auto_selected
    volume_changed
    transition_skipped
    queue_reordered
    liked
    disliked

This allows the system to learn distinctions such as:

    "The user does not dislike Track B.
     The user dislikes the transition from A to B."

This is substantially richer than conventional recommendation feedback.


======================================================================
16A. PERSONAL SOVEREIGNTY BOUNDARY
======================================================================

"Personal" is not branding. It is the primary ownership, privacy, and trust
boundary of PMN.

A PMN distinguishes at least four data domains:

1. PUBLIC MUSIC KNOWLEDGE

    Examples:

        title
        artist
        album / release
        ISRC
        public artwork
        public lyrics
        BPM
        key
        public release metadata
        public musical structure
        generally shareable analysis of public music

    This data may be replicated, cached, exchanged, and federated according
    to ordinary provenance and rights rules.

2. PERSONAL DATA

    Examples:

        listening history
        skip history
        favorites
        ratings
        personal tags
        personal notes
        personal memories
        user embeddings
        TasteModel
        SequencePreference
        TransitionPreference
        SessionPreference
        ContextPreference
        DiscoveryPreference
        private listening context

    This is first-class privacy-sensitive data.

    Default:

        visibility = PRIVATE_TO_PMN
        cross_pmn_replication = FORBIDDEN
        cross_pmn_read = FORBIDDEN
        export = EXPLICIT_ONLY

3. PRIVATE RESOURCES

    Examples:

        local audio files
        private recordings
        unreleased media
        private lyric corrections
        private generated material
        provider credentials
        authentication tokens
        private models
        private model adaptations

    Private resources remain inside the owning PMN unless an explicit,
    scoped sharing operation permits otherwise.

4. EXPLICIT SHARED DATA

    Examples:

        shared playlist
        explicitly shared recommendation
        shared annotation
        collaborative session state
        shared transition plan
        shared generated mix

    Shared data belongs to an explicit scope.

    It must not silently inherit access to the owner's unrelated personal
    state.


======================================================================
16B. OWNERSHIP APPLIES TO RELATIONS, NOT ONLY OBJECTS
======================================================================

A fact being attached to a public song does not make that fact public.

Example:

    Recording A --performed_by--> Artist B

        class = PublicMusicKnowledge

But:

    User --prefers--> Recording A

        class = PersonalPreference

Likewise:

    Recording A BPM = 128

may be public music knowledge, while:

    User usually skips Recording A after midnight

is personal behavioral data.

Relations therefore require ownership and provenance.

Conceptually:

    Relation {
        subject,
        predicate,
        object,
        owner,
        data_class,
        provenance
    }

The same principle applies to graph edges, events, embeddings, summaries,
scores, and learned parameters.


======================================================================
16C. DERIVED DATA INHERITS PRIVACY
======================================================================

Transforming personal data does not make it non-personal.

Examples:

    Listening History
        ->
    Taste Embedding

The Taste Embedding remains PersonalPreference data.

    Private Recording
        ->
    Audio Embedding

The embedding remains private unless explicitly declassified.

    Skip History + Context
        ->
    Prediction Model

The resulting model remains personal.

Default rule:

    DerivedArtifact.data_class
        = strongest_privacy_class(inputs)

unless an explicit, user-authorized declassification operation exists.

A vector, embedding, score, model, or aggregate must not be treated as
public merely because it no longer resembles the original records.


======================================================================
16D. LOCAL AVAILABILITY IS PERSONAL
======================================================================

Public music metadata and personal possession are different facts.

Example:

    Recording X has BPM 120

may be public.

But:

    Recording X exists as FLAC on Alice's desktop

reveals Alice's private library and infrastructure.

Therefore the following are personal or private by default:

    local library membership
    replica locations
    private cache contents
    private node topology
    exact storage capacity
    private compute hardware
    provider account availability

Federation should expose abstract capability or availability answers only
when necessary for an explicit operation.


======================================================================
16E. COMPUTE TO DATA, NOT PERSONAL DATA TO COMPUTE
======================================================================

Cross-PMN operations should prefer:

    Request
        ->
    Local Policy Check
        ->
    Local Computation
        ->
    Minimal Response

over:

    Export Personal Data
        ->
    Remote Computation

Example: group recommendation.

Wrong default:

    Alice TasteModel -> Federation Planner
    Bob TasteModel   -> Federation Planner

Preferred default:

    Federation Planner
        ->
    candidate references
        ->
    Alice PMN performs private local evaluation
        ->
    returns minimal result

The minimal result may be:

    Accept / Neutral / Reject

or a narrowly scoped score or ranking.

The full TasteModel, listening history, and personal embeddings remain
inside Alice's PMN.

The architectural principle is:

    Bring computation to personal data.
    Do not bring personal data to computation by default.


======================================================================
16F. PERSONAL MODELS NEVER BECOME AMBIENT FEDERATION STATE
======================================================================

The following remain inside the PMN boundary by default:

    TasteModel
    UserEmbedding
    BehaviorEmbedding
    TransitionPreference
    SequencePreference
    SessionPreference
    ContextModel
    DiscoveryModel
    PersonalMemory

A foreign PMN must never receive ambient visibility into another PMN's
personal state.

There is no generic cross-PMN API equivalent to:

    browse_user_state()
    read_full_history()
    export_taste_model()

Cross-boundary interaction must occur through explicit operations such as:

    explicit_share
    explicit_query
    explicit_session_participation
    explicit_capability_lease

Preference projections are themselves personal data and must not be exported
by default.


======================================================================
16G. SHARED EVENTS AND PERSONAL EVENTS ARE SEPARATE
======================================================================

The same real-world listening event may produce different projections.

Example:

    Track X was played in Shared Session S.

Shared projection:

    Session S played Track X at time T.

Alice's private projection:

    listened 80%
    reduced volume
    disliked transition
    skipped shortly afterward

Bob's private projection:

    completed track
    favorited track
    replayed later

SharedSpace receives only the shared projection.

Each participant's private reaction remains inside their own PMN.

This separation should be structural rather than relying only on field-level
access control.


======================================================================
16H. SAME-PMN AND CROSS-PMN TRUST ARE DIFFERENT
======================================================================

Same-PMN nodes operate inside one personal trust domain.

Subject to the owner's policy, they may replicate:

    personal history
    personal models
    personal memory
    private analysis
    private lyrics
    private media

A different PMN is a foreign trust domain.

Cross-PMN defaults are restricted to:

    PublicMusicKnowledge
    +
    ExplicitSharedData

Any wider disclosure requires explicit authorization.

Therefore:

    same PMN
        = personal trust domain

    different PMN
        = foreign trust domain


======================================================================
16I. DATA POLICY AS A FIRST-CLASS TYPE
======================================================================

Privacy classification should exist in the data model, not only in UI text.

A conceptual model:

    DataClass {
        PublicMusicKnowledge,

        PersonalBehavior,
        PersonalPreference,
        PersonalMemory,

        PrivateMedia,
        PrivateCredential,
        PrivateModel,

        SharedArtifact,
        SharedSessionState
    }

And:

    DataPolicy {
        class,
        owner,
        scope,
        exportable,
        replicable,
        computable_remotely,
        declassification_policy
    }

Examples:

    BPM:
        class = PublicMusicKnowledge
        replicable = true

    SkipHistory:
        class = PersonalBehavior
        replicable = same_pmn_only

    TasteEmbedding:
        class = PersonalPreference
        replicable = same_pmn_only

    ProviderCredential:
        class = PrivateCredential
        replicable = false

    SharedPlaylist:
        class = SharedArtifact
        scope = SharedSpace(S)


======================================================================
16J. PRIVACY TAINT THROUGH AI AND ANALYSIS
======================================================================

AI, DSP, analytics, and embeddings must preserve data provenance and privacy
classification.

If a model consumes personal data, outputs derived from that data are
personal by default.

If a model consumes private media, outputs that reveal information about the
media remain private by default.

Privacy classification therefore propagates through:

    AI inference
    embeddings
    recommendation scores
    summaries
    learned parameters
    personal adaptation
    analytics
    generated artifacts

This prevents privacy loss through "representation laundering", where
sensitive source data is converted into a vector or model and incorrectly
treated as harmless.


======================================================================
16K. PMN PRIVACY PRINCIPLE
======================================================================

Formal statement:

    Personal means that behavioral data, preference data, memory, private
    resources, credentials, and any representations derived from them remain
    inside the owner's PMN trust boundary unless the owner explicitly
    delegates a narrowly scoped disclosure or operation.

Federation must therefore be based on:

    explicit scope
    minimum disclosure
    local evaluation where possible
    revocable delegation
    provenance
    ownership
    purpose limitation

A PMN that exposes personal state to foreign PMNs by default is no longer a
Personal Music Network in the architectural sense.


======================================================================
17. NETWORK MODEL: ONE DEVICE AND MANY DEVICES
======================================================================

The network design begins with two strict rules:

    1. Single-device and multi-device PMN must share the same logical model.

    2. Physical nodes are not the primary scheduling abstraction.
       Capabilities are.

A single-device PMN may physically contain only one node, but logically the
same planning machinery still exists:

    Logical PMN
        ->
    Capability Requirements
        ->
    Capability Planner
        ->
    Local Capability Instances

A multi-device PMN uses exactly the same semantics:

    Logical PMN
        ->
    Capability Requirements
        ->
    Capability Planner
        ->
    Placement Graph
        ->
    Network Scheduler
        ->
    Capability Instances distributed across many Nodes

The number and type of physical devices change deployment, not meaning.


======================================================================
18. NODE: IDENTITY, RESOURCE BOUNDARY, AND CAPABILITY CONTAINER
======================================================================

A Node is not fundamentally "the thing that gets a task".

A Node is primarily:

    - an identity boundary
    - a trust boundary
    - a failure boundary
    - a resource boundary
    - a locality boundary
    - a container for capability instances

A node should therefore not be classified primarily as:

    Phone
    Desktop
    Server
    NAS

Those are product forms, not architectural roles.

Conceptually:

    Node {
        identity,
        capability_inventory,
        resources,
        presence,
        connectivity,
        trust,
        policy,
        constraints
    }

A desktop may host:

    local storage capability
    FLAC decode capability
    AAC encode capability
    realtime DSP capability
    CUDA AI inference capability
    music analysis capability
    timeline compilation capability
    audio sink capability
    desktop UI capability

A phone may host:

    local storage capability
    decode capability
    mobile UI capability
    sensor/context capability
    small-model inference capability
    headphone audio sink capability

A NAS may host:

    object storage capability
    replication capability

A speaker may host:

    audio sink capability

A watch may host:

    control capability

A GPU server may host:

    AI inference capability
    stem separation capability
    analysis capability

The architecture should therefore avoid reasoning such as:

    "AI goes to the desktop."
    "Storage goes to the NAS."
    "Playback goes to the phone."

It should instead reason about capability requirements and currently
available capability instances.


======================================================================
19. CAPABILITY INSTANCE: THE PRIMARY EXECUTION ABSTRACTION
======================================================================

The true schedulable execution primitive is CapabilityInstance.

A capability should not be represented only as a boolean.

Conceptually:

    CapabilityInstance {
        id,
        node_id,
        kind,
        version,
        properties,
        limits,
        current_state,
        cost_model,
        endpoint
    }

Examples:

    audio.decode
        codec = FLAC
        max_channels = 8
        max_sample_rate = 384000
        realtime_factor = 45

    audio.transcode
        input = FLAC
        output = AAC
        realtime_factor = 12.4

    audio.output
        channels = 2
        max_sample_rate = 192000
        exclusive = true

    ai.inference
        model = pmn-transition-v3
        backend = CUDA
        vram = 16 GiB
        estimated_latency = 280 ms

    storage.object
        free_space = 5.7 TiB
        persistent = true

    music.analysis.beat
        algorithm = beat-v2
        version = 2.1

The node tells PMN where the capability lives.
The capability tells PMN what can actually be done.


======================================================================
20. CAPABILITY INVENTORY AND DYNAMIC STATE
======================================================================

Capability description has two parts:

Static properties:

    supported codecs
    supported model families
    maximum channel count
    supported operations
    hardware features
    implementation version

Dynamic properties:

    current load
    free memory
    free storage
    battery level
    temperature
    current queue depth
    measured throughput
    current availability
    estimated latency

This distinction matters because:

    "The node supports AI inference"

does not imply:

    "The node is currently the best place to execute this inference."

Capability planning must use both capability semantics and live resource
state.


======================================================================
21. CAPABILITY REQUIREMENTS
======================================================================

Logical music operations produce CapabilityRequirements.

A requirement should be able to express:

    kind
    semantic constraints
    performance constraints
    quality constraints
    resource constraints
    locality constraints
    timing constraints
    privacy constraints

Requirements should also have strength:

    REQUIRED
    PREFERRED
    OPTIONAL

Example:

    Play a 24/96 FLAC in bit-perfect mode.

    REQUIRED:
        audio.decode.flac
        audio.output
        sample_rate >= 96000
        bit_depth >= 24
        no_resampling
        no_sample_format_conversion

    PREFERRED:
        exclusive_output

Example:

    Generate a transition bridge.

    REQUIRED:
        ai.inference
        model = pmn-transition-v3
        memory >= 4 GiB

    PREFERRED:
        latency < 500 ms

    OPTIONAL:
        GPU acceleration

This turns feature support into explicit constraints rather than platform
special cases.


======================================================================
22. CAPABILITY COMPOSITION
======================================================================

A task does not need to be executable by one node.

Capabilities may be composed across nodes.

Example:

    Prepare Track X for playback on Phone.

Required logical pipeline:

    storage.read
        ->
    audio.decode.flac
        ->
    audio.encode.aac
        ->
    network.send
        ->
    audio.decode.aac
        ->
    audio.output

A valid placement may be:

    NAS
        storage.read
            |
            v
    Desktop
        decode.flac
        encode.aac
            |
            v
    Phone
        decode.aac
        audio.output

The planning question is therefore not:

    Which node should run this task?

It is:

    Which capability placement graph best realizes this logical task graph?


======================================================================
23. CAPABILITY EQUIVALENCE AND DEGRADATION
======================================================================

Different capabilities may satisfy the same logical requirement at different
quality or cost levels.

Example:

    Required logical function:
        time_stretch

Possible implementations:

    hardware DSP
    native CPU algorithm
    GPU implementation
    remote server implementation

Likewise a playback requirement may permit:

    FLAC native decode
    server-side FLAC -> AAC transcode
    provider AAC variant
    cached AAC replica

The planner therefore needs explicit equivalence and degradation semantics.

A capability requirement may specify:

    exact requirement
    acceptable alternatives
    ordered fallback set
    forbidden transformations

This is especially important for:

    bit-perfect mode
    lossless requirements
    spatial audio
    low-latency playback
    battery-saving mode
    cellular data policies

Graceful degradation should emerge from planning rules, not hard-coded
device branches.


======================================================================
24. FOUR NETWORK PLANES
======================================================================

PMN networking is decomposed into four planes:

    1. Control Plane
    2. Data Plane
    3. Compute Plane
    4. Time Plane

Capability-oriented execution planning spans all four planes.

The planes answer different questions:

    Control:
        What should happen?

    Data:
        Where are the required artifacts and how can they move?

    Compute:
        Which capability instances should execute the required operations?

    Time:
        When must those operations occur?


======================================================================
25. CONTROL PLANE
======================================================================

The Control Plane carries logical state and intent.

It may contain:

    Music Graph state
    Playback Session state
    SessionGraph
    Timeline Plan
    User Intent
    Capability Requirements
    Node Directory
    Capability Directory
    Resource Availability
    Policies
    Operation Log

When the user taps Play on a phone, the logical operation should resemble:

    PlayIntent {
        recording: X,
        presentation_target: headphones
    }

The control plane should not directly encode:

    "Fetch this URL from Desktop A and execute decoder B."

That is a placement decision made after capability resolution.


======================================================================
26. DATA PLANE
======================================================================

The Data Plane moves artifacts.

Artifacts include:

    audio
    artwork
    lyrics
    analysis results
    waveforms
    embeddings
    AI models
    generated transitions
    timeline assets

The network may track:

    Artifact X

    Desktop       COMPLETE
    Server        COMPLETE
    Phone         PARTIAL
    Provider      REMOTE

The planner asks:

    What artifacts are required?
    Where are they?
    What capability instances need them?
    What transfer path minimizes total execution cost?


======================================================================
27. PARTIAL ARTIFACTS
======================================================================

Availability must not be binary.

Useful states include:

    NONE
    METADATA_ONLY
    PARTIAL
    COMPLETE
    STALE
    UNAVAILABLE

A phone can begin playback with only the first chunks of a large file.

This unifies:

    streaming
    caching
    downloading
    replication

as one data-plane mechanism.


======================================================================
28. CONTENT-ADDRESSED MEDIA
======================================================================

Media storage should preferably support content addressing.

Conceptually:

    ContentHash(audio bytes)
        ->
    ObjectId

Paths become node-local locators, not identities.

This enables:

    deduplication
    integrity verification
    replication
    resume
    cross-node equivalence
    partial transfer

Large media can be represented as chunked manifests:

    MediaManifest
        |- Chunk 0
        |- Chunk 1
        |- Chunk 2
        ...

Chunking allows:

    fast startup
    seek-before-complete
    partial caching
    efficient synchronization


======================================================================
29. COMPUTE PLANE
======================================================================

Music workloads may include:

    decode
    encode
    transcode
    resample
    DSP
    spatial rendering
    BPM analysis
    key detection
    beat tracking
    stem separation
    embedding generation
    lyric recognition
    AI inference
    model adaptation
    transition generation
    timeline compilation

The Compute Plane does not schedule "tasks onto devices" directly.

It schedules task requirements onto capability instances.

Single-node:

    TaskGraph
        ->
    Capability Planner
        ->
    Capability instances on Local Node

Multi-node:

    TaskGraph
        ->
    Capability Planner
        ->
    Placement Graph
        ->
    Capability instances across multiple Nodes


======================================================================
30. TASK GRAPH AND REQUIREMENT GRAPH
======================================================================

Complex music operations naturally form DAGs.

Example:

    Audio
      |- Beat Analysis
      |- Key Analysis
      |- Stem Analysis
      |- Embedding
             |
             v
      Transition Planner
             |
             v
         Timeline IR

Each task node derives capability requirements.

The result is a RequirementGraph:

    Task
        ->
    CapabilityRequirement
        ->
    Candidate CapabilityInstances

The planner then constructs a PlacementGraph that binds each logical
requirement to one or more concrete capability instances.


======================================================================
31. CAPABILITY PLANNER
======================================================================

The Capability Planner is the core abstraction between musical planning and
physical networking.

It consumes:

    Logical Timeline
    Task Graph
    Capability Requirements
    Capability Directory
    Artifact Directory
    Node Resource State
    Link State
    User Policy
    Privacy Policy
    Power Policy
    Quality Policy
    Timing Constraints

It produces:

    PlacementGraph

A PlacementGraph answers:

    Which capability instance executes each operation?
    Which artifacts must be moved?
    Which intermediate results are materialized?
    Which operations remain local?
    Which operations may be fused?
    Which operations may be delegated?


======================================================================
32. PLACEMENT AS AN OPTIMIZATION PROBLEM
======================================================================

Placement is a constrained optimization problem.

A conceptual objective may minimize:

    total latency
    + transfer cost
    + compute cost
    + energy cost
    + monetary cost
    + reliability risk
    + quality loss
    + privacy exposure

subject to:

    capability requirements
    resource limits
    connectivity
    timing constraints
    user policy
    privacy policy
    media rights
    quality requirements

Weights can change dynamically.

Examples:

    Phone battery is low:
        energy weight increases.

    Cellular network is active:
        transfer-cost weight increases.

    User requests lowest latency:
        latency weight increases.

    Private lyric analysis:
        cloud capability instances are forbidden.

    Bit-perfect playback:
        resampling and format-conversion placements are forbidden.


======================================================================
33. NETWORK SCHEDULER
======================================================================

The Network Scheduler takes a PlacementGraph and makes it executable.

It resolves:

    transport selection
    artifact transfer
    task start order
    dependency readiness
    reservations
    buffering
    retries
    failover
    time synchronization
    execution endpoints

It produces:

    DistributedExecutionGraph

The Capability Planner answers:

    "What should execute where, in capability terms?"

The Network Scheduler answers:

    "How do those placements actually run now?"


======================================================================
34. TIME PLANE
======================================================================

Music networking has a requirement ordinary file sync does not:

    Correct timing matters.

For:

    synchronized speakers
    multi-room playback
    remote playback
    cross-device handoff
    distributed realtime DSP

the system may need to estimate:

    clock offset
    RTT
    jitter
    clock drift

PMN therefore needs a logical timing model.

A playback instruction may be expressed as:

    "Execute Timeline position 123.000 s
     at PMN time T."

Timing capability itself may be modeled explicitly:

    clock.sync
    realtime.schedule
    low_jitter_output

A synchronized group can therefore be planned according to actual timing
capabilities rather than merely node presence.


======================================================================
35. THREE DISTINCT MULTI-DEVICE PLAYBACK MODES
======================================================================

These must not be conflated.

35.1 Handoff

    Desktop:
        Track A @ 1:23.500

    -> transfer session semantics ->

    Phone:
        Track A @ 1:23.500

The original playback endpoint stops.

Prefer semantic handoff:

    recording
    position
    session graph
    policies
    transition state

The destination resolves the required capabilities and media independently.

Only when the destination cannot obtain the content should peer streaming be
required.


35.2 Remote Playback

    Phone:
        control capability

    Desktop:
        timeline / decode / DSP capabilities

    Speaker:
        audio output capability

The audio does not need to pass through the controller.


35.3 Synchronized Group

    One logical PlaybackSession
        ->
    several audio.output capability instances

All outputs execute a shared timeline under timing constraints.


======================================================================
36. PLAYBACK SESSION
======================================================================

PlaybackSession is a logical network entity.

Conceptually:

    PlaybackSession {
        id,
        session_graph,
        timeline_state,
        current_position,
        current_musical_region,
        policy,
        presentation_targets
    }

A presentation target should not necessarily be a node ID.

It may be a logical requirement such as:

    "my headphones"
    "living room group"
    "current mobile device"
    "any bit-perfect sink"

The capability planner resolves that target into concrete output capability
instances.


======================================================================
37. SESSION AUTHORITY
======================================================================

Synchronized playback requires authoritative session state.

One capability instance may provide:

    session.control.authority

Responsibilities include:

    timeline decisions
    play/pause
    seek
    session position
    transition decisions

Other participants follow the authoritative timeline.

Authority itself should be capability-oriented:

    If the current authority disappears, another eligible capability instance
    may assume the role according to policy.

For a small personal network, a lease and deterministic priority system may
be sufficient. A heavyweight distributed-consensus protocol is not
automatically required.


======================================================================
38. STATE CLASSES
======================================================================

Not all state should synchronize.

PMN should distinguish at least:

Persistent State:
    favorites
    playlists
    playback history
    custom metadata
    custom lyrics
    user knowledge

Session State:
    current playback
    current session graph
    current position
    active presentation targets
    current timeline plan

Node-Local State:
    hover state
    animation state
    local buffer fill
    temporary UI state
    thermal state
    device-specific settings

Capability-Local State:
    decoder warm state
    model residency
    GPU pipeline cache
    current DSP state
    reserved realtime buffers

This separation prevents synchronization from becoming indiscriminate.


======================================================================
39. OPERATION-BASED REPLICATION
======================================================================

PMN should avoid synchronizing raw database files.

Prefer:

    Operation
        ->
    Replication
        ->
    Reducer
        ->
    Local Materialized View

Example operations:

    Favorite(recording)
    PlaylistInsert(playlist, recording)
    PlaylistRemove(playlist, entry)
    CustomLyricsUpdate(recording)
    PlaybackCompleted(recording)

SQLite can remain the local query/index engine while the operation log
defines replicated semantic change.


======================================================================
40. CONSISTENCY MODEL
======================================================================

Different state deserves different consistency.

Examples:

    Media Object:
        immutable

    Analysis Artifact:
        immutable + versioned

    Playback Event:
        append-only

    Favorite:
        eventually consistent

    Tags:
        mergeable set

    Playlist:
        ordered merge

    Current Session:
        authority-driven

    Capability telemetry:
        ephemeral / eventually refreshed

    Buffer state:
        local only

    Credentials:
        explicitly scoped

PMN should not pursue global strong consistency where the domain does not
require it.


======================================================================
41. OFFLINE-FIRST CAPABILITY MODEL
======================================================================

Nodes being offline is normal.

Capabilities therefore appear and disappear dynamically.

Typical topology:

    laptop sleeping
    phone outside home
    home server online
    desktop shut down

The planner must react to changing capability availability.

Example:

    Required:
        storage.read(Recording X)
        audio.decode
        audio.output

Available:

    Phone:
        output available
        no media

    Desktop:
        lossless storage available
        node offline

    Server:
        AAC artifact available
        online

    Provider:
        FLAC available
        online

The planner may choose the server AAC path now and re-evaluate future plans
when the desktop returns.


======================================================================
42. ARTIFACT PLACEMENT POLICY
======================================================================

Capability placement and artifact placement are related but distinct.

Capability placement answers:

    Where should computation execute?

Artifact placement answers:

    Where should data persist or be cached?

Examples:

    Home Server:
        retain all lossless media

    Phone:
        cache favorite playlists
        maximum 30 GB

    Laptop:
        metadata everywhere
        recent media only

Placement can become predictive:

    The user is likely to listen during tomorrow's commute.
    The phone is charging on Wi-Fi tonight.
    Replicate likely-needed artifacts now.

This is a PMN-specific extension beyond ordinary file synchronization.


======================================================================
43. DIRECTORIES
======================================================================

Planning requires several logical directories:

    NodeDirectory
    CapabilityDirectory
    ArtifactDirectory
    LinkDirectory

Example capability entries:

    ai.inference / pmn-transition-v3

        Desktop GPU:
            available
            latency 280 ms

        Home Server CPU:
            available
            latency 4.8 s

        Phone:
            unsupported

Example artifact entries:

    FLAC Object ABC

        Desktop SSD:
            complete
            local latency 0.2 ms

        Home Server:
            complete
            LAN latency 4 ms

        Phone:
            unavailable

        Streaming Provider:
            remote
            estimated latency 80 ms

Planning operates over these directories rather than device names alone.


======================================================================
44. LOCAL FAST PATH
======================================================================

Logical uniformity must not force inefficient implementation.

If producer capability and consumer capability are local to the same node,
PMN should use direct local mechanisms:

    memory
    shared buffers
    filesystem
    in-process calls
    local GPU resources

It should not route local playback through localhost RPC merely to preserve a
network abstraction.

The architecture unifies semantics, not overhead.


======================================================================
45. TRANSPORT ABSTRACTION
======================================================================

Upper layers should not depend on a specific transport.

Possible transport paths include:

    in-process
    shared memory
    LAN
    direct QUIC
    QUIC over WireGuard
    public direct connection
    relay
    USB/tethered link

Conceptually:

    PeerTransport

selects a suitable connection for the DistributedExecutionGraph.

Transport is an implementation detail below capability and placement
semantics.


======================================================================
46. DISCOVERY VS CONNECTIVITY VS CAPABILITY DISCOVERY
======================================================================

These are separate questions.

Node Discovery:

    Which nodes exist?

Connectivity:

    Through which paths can a known node be reached?

Capability Discovery:

    Which capability instances does that node currently expose?

A node can be known but unreachable.

A reachable node can be online while a specific capability is unavailable.

For example:

    Desktop:
        reachable

    CUDA GPU capability:
        unavailable because GPU is reserved

Therefore node presence and capability availability must not be collapsed
into one state.


======================================================================
47. TRUST MODEL
======================================================================

Local network membership does not imply trust.

Each node should have a cryptographic identity.

Example:

    node keypair
    NodeId = hash(public key)

Enrollment can use:

    QR code
    one-time secret
    mutual verification

After enrollment, trusted node membership is independent of current IP
address or network location.

Capability delegation must additionally respect authorization.

A trusted node does not automatically receive permission to exercise every
capability or credential in the PMN.

Trust is scoped by boundary:

    same-PMN trust
        may authorize personal-state replication according to owner policy

    cross-PMN trust
        never implies access to personal state

A foreign PMN receives only explicitly shared scopes and explicitly delegated
operations.


======================================================================
48. CREDENTIALS AND DELEGATION
======================================================================

PMN node identity and provider credentials are different objects.

For example:

    PMN Node Identity
    !=
    NetEase session credential

Credentials should support explicit placement and delegation policies:

    LocalOnly
    SelectedNodes
    DelegatedUse

A home server may be allowed to analyze local audio without receiving the
user's provider credential.

Likewise a remote capability may be technically able to execute an
operation but still be excluded by privacy or authorization policy.

Cross-PMN compute is subject to the Personal Sovereignty Boundary.
A technically suitable remote capability must not receive personal data
unless the data policy explicitly permits that disclosure. When possible,
the computation should execute inside the owning PMN and only its minimal
result should cross the boundary.


======================================================================
49. CAPABILITY-ORIENTED EXECUTION PLANNING
======================================================================

The complete physical planning chain is:

    Music Intent
        |
        v
    Music Planner
        |
        v
    Logical Timeline / Task Graph
        |
        v
    Capability Requirement Derivation
        |
        v
    Capability Requirements
        |
        v
    Capability Planner
        |
        v
    Placement Graph
        |
        v
    Network Scheduler
        |
        v
    Distributed Execution Graph
        |
        v
    Capability Instances
        |
        v
    Physical Nodes / Links / Artifacts

This is the central network architecture of PMN.

The system does not ask:

    "Which device should do this?"

until after it has answered:

    "What capabilities are required?"
    "Which concrete capability instances can satisfy them?"
    "Can several capability instances be composed?"
    "What placement best satisfies policy and cost constraints?"


======================================================================
50. EXAMPLE: TRANSITION GENERATION
======================================================================

Logical task:

    GenerateTransition(A, B)

Requirements:

    REQUIRED:
        audio.decode(A)
        audio.decode(B)
        music.analysis.beat
        music.analysis.key
        ai.inference(model = pmn-transition-v3)
        storage.temp >= 500 MiB

    PREFERRED:
        generation_latency < 2 s

Available capability instances:

    Phone:
        decode
        beat analysis
        8 GiB RAM
        battery = 23%
        no transition-generation model

    Desktop:
        decode
        beat analysis
        key analysis
        CUDA transition model
        online

    Home Server:
        decode
        beat analysis
        key analysis
        CPU transition model
        always online

Capability filtering:

    Phone:
        invalid for full task

    Desktop:
        valid

    Home Server:
        valid

Cost evaluation:

    Desktop:
        generation 1.8 s
        transfer low

    Home Server:
        generation 19 s
        transfer medium

Placement result:

    Desktop capability instances selected.

Nothing in the Music Planner needed to know that the selected execution
device was a desktop.

======================================================================
54. CORE ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLES
======================================================================

PMN should preserve the following principles:

1. MUSIC FIRST
   Network architecture exists to serve musical semantics.

2. LOGICAL NETWORK FIRST
   One device and many devices share one logical model.

3. PERSONAL BOUNDARY PRECEDES FEDERATION
   Public music knowledge may cross PMN boundaries. Personal behavior,
   preference, memory, private resources, credentials, and their derived
   representations remain inside the owner PMN by default.

5. CAPABILITY FIRST, DEVICE SECOND
   Planning begins with required capabilities. Physical device identity is a
   placement constraint, not the primary execution abstraction.

5. NODES ARE BOUNDARIES AND CONTAINERS
   A node provides identity, trust, resources, locality, failure isolation,
   and capability instances.

6. CAPABILITIES ARE COMPOSABLE
   One logical operation may be realized by capability instances distributed
   across several nodes.

7. IDENTITY IS NOT LOCATION
   Music identity must not be defined by provider IDs, file paths, or URLs.

8. TRACK IS NOT THE MINIMUM PLAYBACK UNIT
   MusicalSegment is the practical unit for advanced planning.

9. PLAYBACK IS PLANNING + COMPILATION + RENDERING
   It is not merely "decode then output".

10. TRANSITIONS ARE PROGRAMS
   They should be composable over a general timeline algebra.

11. SESSION STRUCTURE MATTERS
    Long-horizon listening should be planned, not merely greedily sequenced.

12. PLAYBACK CAPABILITY CHANGES POSSIBLE MUSICAL PATHS
    A stronger renderer can create transitions that were previously
    infeasible.

13. DATA, COMPUTE, CONTROL, AND TIME ARE DIFFERENT NETWORK PROBLEMS
    They should not be collapsed.

14. CAPABILITY AVAILABILITY IS DYNAMIC
    Supported does not mean currently available or currently optimal.

15. LOCAL EXECUTION MUST REMAIN FAST
    Network abstraction must not force unnecessary RPC or serialization.

16. OFFLINE IS NORMAL
    Temporary node and capability absence are routine states, not exceptional
    failures.

17. STATE CONSISTENCY IS DOMAIN-SPECIFIC
    Not everything needs strong consistency.

18. CREDENTIALS ARE NOT GLOBAL BY DEFAULT
    Access to provider accounts must be explicitly scoped.

19. DEGRADATION IS A PLANNING PROPERTY
    Fallback behavior should be described through requirement alternatives and
    policy, not platform-specific branches.

20. USER FEEDBACK CLOSES THE LOOP
    PMN continuously learns not only what music the user likes, but how the
    user prefers music to be sequenced, transitioned, and experienced.

21. IMPLEMENTATION SCOPE MUST NOT DEFINE ARCHITECTURAL SCOPE
    Features that are expensive or unavailable today may still belong in the
    complete PMN model.


======================================================================
55. CONCISE DEFINITION
======================================================================

Personal Music Network is a user-owned logical music environment that:

    acquires music,
    understands music,
    organizes music,
    plans music,
    transforms music,
    renders music,
    distributes music,
    executes music through available capabilities across one or many nodes,
    and learns from the user's listening behavior.

Its privacy architecture is:

    Public Music Knowledge:
        may be exchanged subject to provenance and rights.

    Personal State:
        remains inside the owner PMN by default.

    Explicit Shared Scope:
        is the only normal bridge across personal boundaries.

    Derived Personal Representations:
        inherit the privacy of their source data.

Its execution architecture is:

    Music Planner:
        decides how music should happen.

    Capability Requirement Layer:
        describes what abilities are required to realize that plan.

    Capability Planner:
        binds abstract requirements to concrete capability instances and
        constructs the placement graph.

    Network Scheduler:
        turns that placement into a live distributed execution graph over
        nodes, links, artifacts, and clocks.

The resulting system is not fundamentally a player with synchronization.

It is a music operating environment whose musical intelligence, playback,
storage, computation, devices, providers, and personal state form one
coherent capability-oriented network.
